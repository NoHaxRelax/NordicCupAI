What last year's healthcare challenge actually was

Elias's hypothesis, checked: this may be last year's healthcare challenge with audio bolted on the front. Ambolt's 2025 repo is public and his own solution is on a branch of bforbanks/dm-i-ai-2025. Both were read. The 2025 healthcare task was:

This settles the retrieval argument, in Elias's favour, on the other side of the pipeline

The measured case against retrieval was about the transcript: a consultation is around 2,050 tokens, it fits in context whole, and chunking it can only discard the context that resolves ambiguity. That still holds.

But last year's task retrieved over a 115-topic reference corpus the organisers handed you, and that is a completely different and entirely justified use of retrieval. 7.1 MB does not fit in any context window. If 2026 follows the same pattern, the knowledge base is where the retrieval layer belongs, and we already own a tuned one.

So the rule is: retrieve over the knowledge, not over the transcript.

What transfers from our 2025 solution

On the elias branch, combined-model-2 is a hybrid retriever: BM25 for exact medical terminology, all-mpnet-base-v2 for semantics, combined by reciprocal rank fusion, chunked on markdown medical headers at 600 words with 100 overlap, top-8 candidates into a single LLM call that answers both fields at once. The LLM was gemma3:27b through Ollama, with 12b and deepseek-r1:32b as switchable alternatives. Sibling branches carry a Bayesian-optimised sweep over the fusion weights and a reranking layer.

Reported at 9.55 s per statement on an M4 Mac against a 5 s budget, with GPU expected to close the gap. If the 5 s limit returns and ASR now sits in front of it, that budget is the thing to design against first. Parakeet-CTC runs 9.4x real time on CPU alone, so a 30-second clip costs about 3 s of the 5 before the language model starts.

Two Ambolt repos, and an honest note about what they do and do not prove

Of Ambolt's ten public repositories, exactly two were touched on 31 August 2026, nine seconds apart, seventeen days before the event: speaches (an OpenAI-compatible speech server, which gained a speaker diarization endpoint upstream in December) and chatterbox-tts-api (local text-to-speech with voice cloning). No other repo shares that date.

What this does not show: neither fork contains a single Ambolt commit. Both are identical to or behind upstream, and both were created in February 2026. The 31 August event changed metadata only, not code, so it was a visibility, description or settings change. It is not evidence of work in progress, and an earlier draft of this page said it was.

What it is still worth: a company forks a TTS engine with voice cloning and a speech server with diarization, seven months before running a speech challenge, and then touches exactly those two a fortnight before it. The cheap inference is that the evaluation audio may be TTS-generated rather than recorded. If so it is clean, one speaker at a time, no cross-talk and no room noise, and every WER number on this page is pessimistic. Plan for it as a likely case, not a confirmed one.

reference/dmiai2025/ holds the 2025 brief, its request and response DTO, and the 115-topic mapping.

Three findings that changed the plan

1. A “medical ASR” model is worse than Whisper base on consultations

google/medasr scores 42.8 WER on PriMock57 against 19.1 for plain whisper-base.en. It was trained on dictation — a clinician speaking alone, formally, to a microphone. A consultation is two people interrupting each other. The register mismatch is fatal.

This is the measured form of the argument against fine-tuning on ICD-10 strings. Our own error analysis says the same thing from the other direction: the most-missed words at every model size were i, is, you, ok, yeah, and, it, that, the, a. Not one medical term. The model is not failing on vocabulary.

2. Whisper is no longer the frontier

nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3 dominates whisper-large-v3 on every axis simultaneously: better average WER (4.86 vs 5.78), better on AMI which is the closest public proxy for two-person conversation (9.42 vs 13.63), better long-form, ~13× the throughput, and a third of the parameters. It also emits native word-level timestamps, which deletes the forced-alignment stage entirely.

My baseline was built on the wrong model. It still stands as a measurement of our harness, but Parakeet is the model to beat.

3. LLM post-correction of the transcript makes things worse

The evidence is consistent and it includes PriMock57 specifically:

The ten-minute check that could delete a whole layer

If the organiser's audio has clinician and patient on separate channels (as PriMock57 does), run per-channel ASR and skip diarization entirely. Zero DER, zero added latency. Check this first.

But: our own measurement says it isn't free. Per-channel scored 0.375–0.423 WER against 0.139 mixed, with high deletions and high insertions at once — the signature of microphone bleed. Both channels transcribe the same speech. Cross-channel energy gating is required before this becomes the easy win it looks like.

Parakeet-CTC matches large-v3 without a GPU. 0.142 against 0.139 is 29 edits apart on 9,738 words, which is a tie. The distinction is that the Whisper row used CUDA and the Parakeet row used fourteen CPU threads, so the two RTF figures are not a race against each other — the point is that one of them needed no graphics card to get there, at a third of the parameters. Its deletion count is also the lowest in the table (671), which matters because deletions are the dominant error here.

And this is the weaker Parakeet. The variant the survey actually recommends, parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3, has a token-and-duration transducer decoder that transformers 5.2.0 does not register — it knows parakeet_ctc and parakeet_encoder only. TDT needs NeMo, or transformers from git. That is a UCloud task with a real environment, not a laptop afternoon, and it is worth doing: TDT scores 9.42 against large-v3's 13.63 on AMI, the closest public benchmark to overlapping two-party conversation.

distil-large-v3 dominates both small.en and base.en outright — better WER and 1.5× faster than small.en. There is no reason to run the small English models.

Deletions exceed substitutions at every size. At large-v3, 690 deletions against 446 substitutions. The model isn't mishearing, it's dropping — VAD discarding short utterances, and overlapping speech being lost. That is the failure mode to attack, not vocabulary.

The other half: writing the note

Transcription is the visible part of this challenge. The note is what a clinician would actually keep, and it is graded on completely different terms. PriMock57 turns out to ship the data needed to calibrate it, in a folder most people never open.

human_eval_data/ is the most valuable thing in the corpus

1,425 notes, each marked up by a clinician: how long they spent fixing it, how many statements were wrong, how many things were missing, and whether each fault was critical. Plus 24 automatic metrics precomputed against four different reference regimes. And, buried in results.csv, five independently written notes per consultation on top of the doctor's own — which is what makes a human ceiling computable at all.

It joins to the rest of the corpus only after fixing an ID mismatch: results.csv zero-pads to three digits (day1_consultation010) while every filename and notes/*.json use two. Joining on the raw string silently drops every consultation numbered 10 and up — 11 of 57 — as an empty reference list rather than an error.

Nobody scores well on this, including doctors

Six people wrote up each consultation. Scoring each against the other five, leave-one-out, gives the ceiling on exactly the metric a leaderboard would use.

Against a single reference, the published systems straddle the human line and the best of them beats it. Two clinicians writing up the same conversation simply do not write the same note.

Half the score is available without listening to the consultation

Take a real doctor's note about a different patient entirely and score it against this consultation's references. Same writer pool, same house style, same shorthand, wrong illness.

Note scored against this consultation's 5 evaluator notesROUGE-L
the doctor's note for this consultation0.235

50% of the score survives swapping the patient out. That half is section headings, stock phrasing and clinical shorthand. Everything reflecting whether a system understood this particular conversation lives in the remaining 0.117 of ROUGE-L.

The strategic reading: if note generation is graded on single-reference ROUGE, the whole field compresses into a band of roughly ±0.02 around a number a human cannot beat, and the metric cannot tell a good system from a mediocre one. Match the house style, get to ~0.18, and spend the remaining hours on transcription — which feeds the note anyway — rather than on note polish that the grader cannot see.

Omissions, not hallucinations

64%of critical errors are omissions, not false statements
4.66critical omissions per generated note
2.63critical incorrect statements per generated note
3.93critical errors in a doctor's own note, marked by another clinician

The instinct with a language model in a clinical setting is to clamp down on invention. The measured failure is under-coverage: notes leave things out roughly twice as often as they make things up, and that is what costs a clinician time. Prompt for recall, use the section list as an explicit checklist, and prefer a slightly long note to a terse one.

The last figure is the calibration that keeps this honest. A doctor's own note, read by a different clinician, still draws 3.93 critical corrections. Zero is not the target, and the ten published systems average 8.1 against that 3.93 — roughly twice a human's error count, and costing 136 seconds of editing against the doctor's 97.

What the 13.9% is actually made of

If the evaluation audio is synthesised, our WER is the wrong number to plan against. So we rendered the same six PriMock57 transcripts through TTS and re-scored them. Same words, same references, same scorer, only the acoustics change, which is what makes the difference attributable. Three conditions, and the middle one is the whole point.

ConditionWERno-bcsub delinscontent recall
real recording, mixed down0.1390.131 4466902130.909
TTS voices at the original timestamps0.1220.108 2186583170.901
TTS voices, sequential, no overlap0.0430.046 101352860.959

The timed condition preserves every pause, interruption and overlap the humans actually produced: 112 seconds of two-speaker overlap per consultation. So the first row minus the second is what microphones and rooms cost, and the second minus the third is what overlap costs.

Deletions are overlap, essentially all of it

Holding one factor fixed and changing the otherdeletions
swap real microphones for clean synthetic audio, overlap unchanged 690 → 658, −5%
remove the overlap, voices unchanged 658 → 35, −95%

Earlier this dossier said deletions were the failure mode to attack and blamed "VAD discarding short utterances, and overlapping speech being lost". The VAD half was already refuted by ablation (0.139 either way). This settles the rest: overlap is the entire story. Whisper drops what is spoken over, and audio quality barely touches it.

Substitutions behave the opposite way, halving from 446 to 218 on clean audio at unchanged overlap. So the two error types have two different causes: substitutions are audio quality, deletions are overlap. Any technique aimed at "deletions" that does not address simultaneous speech is aimed at nothing.

Overlap costs 0.079 WER against recording quality's 0.016, a factor of five. If the organisers render dialogue turns sequentially, which is the cheap way to build it, we should expect roughly 4% WER and 96% content recall and the ASR workstream is finished before it starts.

Accented English is five times more expensive than clean English

Pointing the Danish voices at the English transcripts, still scored against the English reference, gives heavily Danish-accented English. That is a real scenario, since a Nordic championship may well feature Nordic speakers working in English, and it is much harder than either arm above.

Sequential, no overlap, same six consultationsWER subdelcontent recall
UK voices0.043101350.959
Danish voices, English words0.2199676430.806

Accent costs more than overlap and recording quality combined, and unlike them it also eats content recall, dropping it below the real-recording baseline of 0.909. Note the error shape: substitutions rise almost tenfold while deletions rise from 35 to 643, so accent damages both channels where overlap damaged only deletions and recording quality only substitutions.

Compare the sequential rows and not the timed ones. The Danish voices take longer to say the same English words, so placing them at the original timestamps produces 174 seconds of overlap per consultation against the UK arm's 112. The timed comparison therefore confounds accent with extra overlap; the sequential rows have zero overlap on both sides and isolate accent cleanly.

This arm was first run under a --danish flag and reported as a Danish-language result. It is not: the words and the reference are English throughout and only the voice is Danish. The genuine Danish arm translates the transcript first, so the Danish text is both what gets spoken and what gets scored, which is the regime a generated challenge lives in and which also means translation errors move both sides together rather than flattering the score.

Four honest limits on that table

  1. The TTS reads the reference transcript verbatim, so there is no pronunciation ambiguity at all. For TTS-generated evaluation data this is exactly representative, because the generated text is the ground truth. As a proxy for recorded speech it is optimistic.
  2. One voice pair, en-GB-RyanNeural and en-GB-SoniaNeural. Voice diversity is untested, and if the organisers used voice cloning their speakers will vary far more.
  3. We rendered with edge-tts, not with Chatterbox, which is the engine they actually forked.
  4. Insertions rise in both clean conditions, 213 to 317 and 286, and we have not explained why. Most likely the TTS voicing written disfluencies ("um", "uh") that the human recording delivered differently. It is the one number in the table we cannot account for.

Does the meaning survive 13.9% WER? Yes.

WER weights every token the same, so losing two hundred "yeah"s scores like losing every drug name. Half our substitution-and-deletion mass sits on twenty function words, so the headline number may be almost entirely noise nobody grades. The test that matters is whether the clinically meaningful words survive. Measured on the same six consultations, scripts/term_recall.py:

0.981red flag words survive (ambulance, chest, bleeding): 51 of 52
0.932temporal expressions, the follow-up intervals: 109 of 117
0.909all clinical content: 220 of 242
0.743drug names, the only weak category: 26 of 35

And the sharper version: of the consultations where the gold diagnosis word is spoken aloud, 3 of 3 survived and none were lost.

In half the sample the diagnosis is never said out loud

Three of six clinicians wrote "viral URTI", "UTI" and "heart failure exacerbation" having uttered none of those phrases. No ASR quality recovers a word nobody spoke. For those cases the task is inference from symptoms, not transcription.

Which settles the allocation question: this challenge is inference-limited, not transcription-limited. Whisper large-v3 out of the box already delivers 91% of the clinical content, and further ASR work competes for hours against the half of the problem where transcription is irrelevant by construction.

Contextual biasing is actively harmful here, which reverses our earlier ranking

This was ranked fifth by expected value per hour on the strength of published results. We measured it and it is the worst thing we tried.

ConditionWERcontent recallwhat happened
plain decode0.1390.909the baseline
initial_prompt, a fluent sentence0.1350.897 0.4 WER points better on a metric made of backchannel, 1.2 points worse on the metric we care about
hotwords, 94 terms0.5280.599a third of each transcript missing
hotwords, 25 terms0.8580.219
hotwords, 10 terms0.6480.413output collapses to 196 words

The damage is not list-size overflow, because it is not monotonic in list size. faster-whisper injects hotwords as prompt context, and the model continues the word list rather than treating it as a vocabulary hint. The 10-term output ends:

labyrinthitis lumbar fasciitis lumbar fasciitis labyrinthitis labyrinthitis
labyrinthitis labyrinthitis levothyroxine levothyroxine levothyroxine

Fewer and rarer terms bias harder and collapse faster, which is why 10 terms beat 94 for sheer damage. Do not pass hotwords to faster-whisper. If a term list is needed, apply it after decoding as a fuzzy-match repair over the hypothesis, where a bad suggestion cannot destroy the transcript.
