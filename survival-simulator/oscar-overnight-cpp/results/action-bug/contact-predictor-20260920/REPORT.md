# Predictive burst contact: local mypc results

The final predictor transferred **34/34 bursts** on four untouched seeds, compared with **25/45** for the existing proximity trigger. Mean score increased from **7,576 to 9,872 (+30.3%)**. Mean survival increased only from **1,248.2 to 1,269.4 seconds**: the score gain is predominantly successful transfer payout, not a comparable increase in lifetime.

All games used the native C++ simulator and unchanged native `pred_best.json` base policy on mypc. Python orchestrates the games and runs the observation-only harvester. No competition API was called, no VM was changed, and nothing was deployed or pushed by this task. These uncapped local scores are not proposed validation submissions.

## Implementation

Opt-in `sacrifice_mode="predict_contact"` in `nightsim/serve/harvest.py`; defaults remain unchanged. See `nightsim/serve/contact_predictor.md` for integration.

- Register observer motion using static edges and associate consecutive predator sightings.
- Reject stationary, ambiguous and stale tracks, including predators already frozen by earlier transfers.
- Predict both the unseen prior predator step and pending contact, accounting for heading, turn limits, competing prey, walking speed, target-biome slowdown and remembered walls.
- Require a one-unit contact margin and reject likely list-skip failures.
- Confirm transfers from subsequent public score change plus disappearance, then retain conservative resting-position memory.
- Preserve the concurrent API agent's optional payout-limit integration. That limit bounds predicted transfer points only; overall survival and food points still require the surrounding score guard.

The predictor reads public observations and prior actions, not engine state, hidden predator energy, seed or full map. Instrumented engine traces are used only to verify outcomes in this diagnostic harness.

## Recorded comparisons

All bursts below use a 200,000-action budget. Other harvester settings: maximum 100 attempts, cooldown 50 seconds, minimum six free agents, trigger distance 30, contact margin 1.0.

Seeds 301–304 became development evidence after their terrain failures informed the final change. Seeds 401–404 were not used for tuning. Across the ten final-version games, transfers were **85/86 (98.8%)**, so the four-seed perfect result is not a guarantee. All 85 final-version native predator-meal outcomes matched the predictor's public-score confirmations. Baseline `public_confirmed=0` means the old selector has no confirmation counter, not that its native transfers failed.

The earlier v3 holdout's survival mean declined from 1,393.4 to 1,373.3 seconds despite its higher score. Individual final-version seeds also regress in score or survival. This is a transfer-reliability improvement, not proof of uniformly longer survival.

## Residual failure and performance

The remaining final-version miss is seed 108 at 1,234.3 seconds. Predicted contact was within range, but a wall never seen by the observer redirected the predator: the actual end distance was about 21.05 rather than the unobstructed 13.62. Retaining previously seen walls cannot resolve genuinely unseen geometry. Hidden terrain, prey and predator state remain general limitations.

In the final untouched holdout, per-game p99 admission latency was 1.18–4.06 ms, but the maximum observed admission was 287 ms. Selected regression runs reached 415 ms. These are local measurements under concurrent simulation and recording, not an HTTP capacity test or proof of a server timing budget. Burst transport, serialization and API limits remain outside this task.
