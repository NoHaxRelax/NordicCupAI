# No-trapping campaign: findings, 2026-09-19

What was measured, what was wrong with how we were measuring it, and what is left
to try. Written for whoever continues this campaign.

**The short version.** Twenty-six policy variants were measured at 500 paired maps
and **not one is significantly better than the default**. The single validated
improvement remains a held-out result of **+94.1 points (t = 3.33)** from the
earlier study campaign. Most of today's value is in the defects found in the
measurement apparatus - several of which were silently producing fake results -
and in narrowing where the score actually is.

---

## 1. The measurement was broken in four independent ways

Each of these produced confident-looking numbers that meant nothing.

### 1.1 Five parameters could not affect anything

`fastsim/_orchard_policy.cpp` resolves config keys by name and silently drops the
rest, and the native policy had no implementation of the `wall_*` deflection
block. Under `--engine native` - which is what the whole campaign runs -
`wall_mode`, `wall_engage_r`, `wall_target_gap`, `wall_epsilon` and
`steer_max_ticks` did nothing at all.

The damage is worse than wasted trials. **An inert probe returns its parent's
score exactly**, so the study records it as tying the best-known configuration.
In the campaign-1 data, 515 trials touched an inert parameter and 216 of those
changed nothing else and tied their parent to the last digit. Those register as
wins and feed evolution's parentage as real findings. A per-parameter table built
from that data showed `wait_tol` at 21/21 wins with the highest median gain in
the whole search - for a parameter that cannot act.

Fixed: the layer is ported (`fastsim/_evasion.hpp`), verified bit-identical.

### 1.2 The parity verifier only ever checked default values

`fastsim/verify_evasion.py` compared the Python and native policies at the
**default** configuration. At the default, `wall_mode = 0` and the whole feature
is off, so the two agree no matter what native implements. That is precisely how
five missing parameters passed every check, on every deploy, for the entire
campaign.

Fixed: `--wall-modes` and `--set KEY=VALUE` verify a mechanism at its *on* value,
steer counters are reported, and a deflection mode that never actually steered is
now a **failure** rather than a pass - agreement on a branch that never ran
proves nothing.

### 1.3 Two parameters were searched over ranges that cannot reach them

`models/notrap_config.py` derives a range as `(.5*v, 2*v)` and falls back to
`(0., 1.)` when the default is `0`. So `cluster_radius`, a **world-unit radius**
that needs 30-150 to group neighbouring trees, was searched over `[0, 1]`; and
`nursery_bonus`, added to a score compared against `site_min = 5.0`, was capped at
`1.0` where it can never change a decision. Both features exist in Python *and*
C++ and had never been evaluated in 2410 trials.

Fixed with explicit ranges. They have now been measured - see §3.

### 1.4 Results are not comparable across CPU generations

A byte-identical binary with a byte-identical config and the same seeds produces
**different games** on Zen2/Zen3 than on Zen4/Zen5. Full write-up in
`docs/cpu_reproducibility_20260919.md`. This invalidated one sweep outright and
explains the previously-unexplained two-baseline split in the campaign-1 study
data (14 pods scoring the default at 1545.1, 9 at 1651.0).

Fixed: sweeps are restricted to one CPU family by default.

### 1.5 Two smaller ones

- **`trial['changed']` is parent-relative, not default-relative.** It names what
  moved versus the trial's parent, so on a 14-deep chain it reported one changed
  parameter while eleven actually differed. Any statement of the form "the winner
  is a single-knob change" read from that field is wrong.
- **A dead study reported success.** `deploy_cpp.py` fired a detached launch and
  printed `LAUNCHED` without checking it survived. All 21 studies died instantly on
  an argument error (`--train-seeds 0..499` collides with the default validation
  seeds `[101, 102, 103]`) and the fleet sat idle while the log said otherwise.
  Fixed: the deploy now waits, greps for the live process, and reports
  `STUDY_DIED` with the console tail.

## 2. What the statistics actually support

Measured from a 200-map held-out comparison, not assumed:

- Score std across maps is **~372**; the paired between-config correlation is
  **rho = 0.43**, so sigma of the paired difference is **~399**. Pairing helps far
  less than a shared seed list suggests.
- Minimum detectable effect (80% power, alpha = 0.05):

  | maps/trial | paired SE | smallest detectable gap |
  |---|---|---|
  | 50 | 56.4 | **158** |
  | 200 | 28.2 | 79 |
  | 500 | 17.8 | **50** |
  | 1000 | 12.6 | 35 |

- **At 50 maps the MDE is 158 points.** The real +94 effect we later confirmed was
  *invisible* at that sample size. Every ranking decision the 2410-trial campaign
  made was noise-dominated.
- **Winner's curse:** the maximum of 2702 trials scored at 50 maps sits ~**224
  points** above the default from noise alone. The campaign leader's +145 training
  gap is *below* that line. At 500 maps the same bar is ~71.

Consequences for method: every candidate is now measured on 500 maps, every
comparison is paired against a control on the same CPU family, and nothing is
believed on training data alone.

## 3. Measured results (500 paired maps, seeds 20000+)

Seeds come from a band no training, validation or held-out stage touches, so
nothing here was selected on these maps.

### Predator geometry - `deflect1`, baseline 1485.7 (valid)

| arm | delta | SE | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| wall1_engage130 | -8.0 | 19.3 | -0.42 |
| wall1_default | **-53.8** | 19.7 | -2.73 |
| wall1_eps0.35 | -69.5 | 18.6 | -3.75 |
| wall2_default | **-134.7** | 17.5 | -7.71 |
| evade_closest | **-128.4** | 18.7 | -6.86 |
| evade_closest_wall1 | **-187.4** | 18.8 | -9.97 |
| *pred_r100 (control)* | -67.3 | 19.6 | -3.44 |
| *pred_r50 (control)* | -168.4 | 18.4 | -9.17 |

The controls are the point. `pred_r` is a dial the engine certainly reads, and it
moves the score with large t - so the harness resolves real effects at this sample
size, and the mechanism negatives are true negatives rather than an insensitive
rig.

Two things worth recording. The earlier 40-seed verdict on deflection was
**-52.8**; the properly powered number is **-53.8**. The underpowered estimate was
right, and "underpowered" was not a reason to expect it to be wrong. And
`evade_closest` - analytically the most compelling idea in the set, ranked 2/16 in
a prior tournament, with its predicate already written and float-verified in the
policy - measures **-128**.

### Economy - `econ1`, baseline 1485.7 (valid; `breed250` excluded, ran on a Zen2 pod)

| arm | delta | SE | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| dodge1.05 | **+16.7** | 19.2 | +0.87 |
| nursery5 | -11.5 | 18.7 | -0.61 |
| breed220 | -21.0 | 18.7 | -1.13 |
| breed300 | -34.9 | 19.4 | -1.80 |
| nursery10 | -35.1 | 18.9 | -1.86 |
| cluster30 | -36.5 | 19.9 | -1.84 |
| cluster60 | -40.9 | 19.6 | -2.09 |
| cluster100 | -89.4 | 20.7 | -4.32 |
| cluster150 | **-108.5** | 20.2 | -5.38 |

- **`breed_reserve` does not pay.** The mechanism was sound on paper: spawning
  costs 100, the default reserve of 200 leaves a parent at ~100, and that *is*
  `max_energy/5` for a 500-energy body - the threshold at which
  `environment.py:512` forbids sprinting. So the default parks every new parent
  exactly on the sprint-lock cliff. Raising it does not help, so either the
  sprint-floor hypothesis needs its real implementation (feed locked agents first)
  or it is wrong.
- **`cluster_radius` actively hurts, monotonically.** New information - this
  parameter had never been evaluated. The clustering feature is a liability.
- **`nursery_bonus` is null.** Also never previously evaluated.

### Flee-turn - `flee1` **discarded, and not yet replaced**

Its baseline ran on a Zen3 pod while its arms were spread across both CPU groups,
so the numbers measured the hardware as much as the mechanism. Do not cite the
`flee1` figures; the directory is kept as `flee1-mixed-cpu-INVALID`.

The replacement run (`flee2`, restricted to one CPU family) was in flight when the
fleet stopped and produced nothing. **So the flee-turn mechanism
(`pred_face_threat`) is currently UNMEASURED**, not measured-and-null - the code
is committed and verified, but no trustworthy number exists for it. The same
applies to the population-schedule sweep (`pop1`), which had four arms and no
baseline when it was cut short.

Both are cheap to redo: 13 and 18 configurations respectively, about one pod-hour
each on Zen4/Zen5 hardware.

### Net

**31 arms measured across two valid sweeps, zero significantly positive.** The only thing that has pointed
up is `pred_dodge_ang ~ 1.05` at +16.7 (t = 0.87) - not significant, and it was
the best of several arms. It is worth one clean re-test because that parameter has
never moved from 1.4 in the entire campaign, and the evasion block it belongs to
was imported from a *different engine* and never validated against this one.

## 4. Where the score actually is

Three independent analyses converged, and they explain why every evasion
mechanism failed.

**Score is survival time.** It decomposes exactly as
`sim_time + sum(fruit_energy)/1000 - sum(victim_energy)/100`
(`src/elements/environment.py:757, :671, :723`). Measured over 525 trials / 2100
games, `mean_score - mean_survival` has mean **+2.6** and sd 41.5 - the two
non-time terms are jointly *below* the 50-point detection floor. Optimising score
and optimising time-to-extinction are the same problem. There is no terminal bonus
and nothing is ever banked.

**The horizon is never reached.** 0 of 2100 games reached 3000 s; mean survival
1360, best trial mean 2046. Any mechanism gated later than t ~ 1800 fires in under
2% of games and is unmeasurable by construction. The real "late game" is
t ~ 1200-2000.

**Evasion cannot reach most deaths.** A per-death diagnostic over 192 games found,
of ~148 kills per game:

- **82%** are agents below `max_energy/5`, where the engine **forbids sprinting**
- **76%** are children under 60 s (children spawn at 75 energy against a ~500 max,
  i.e. **sprint-locked from birth**)
- **95%** were already evading
- only 12% could sprint at all

Four fifths of the victims have no speed to steer with. That is the mechanical
reason deflection, corner-steering, escape-search and the flee-turn exploit all
measure negative: they optimise a branch the dying cannot use.

**The food curve sets the clock.** Tree births decay as `0.5**(t/300)` with a ~57 s
lifespan, giving roughly `N_trees ~ N0 * 2^(-t/600)`. Consequently
**delta_score ~ 600 * log2(throughput ratio)** - our 50-point detection floor is a
**6% change in sustainable energy throughput**, and +300 would need +41%.

## 5. Engine defects (found, not used)

### NaN corner anchor - real, reproducible, and not usable

`environment.py:563` applies `turn_angle` with no clamp and no finiteness check;
`:537-538` then feeds it to `cos`/`sin`. A non-finite value makes the position
NaN, `_in_obstacle` returns False (all NaN comparisons are false), and the bounds
clamp at `:777-779` does `max(size, min(W-size, NaN))`, which returns `W-size`
because Python's `min` keeps its first argument. The agent lands on exactly
`(W-size, H-size)`. Identical in C++ (`_engine.cpp:1063-1066`).

A NaN `move_distance` additionally makes **energy NaN**, and `if agent.energy <= 0`
(`:642`) is false forever. Measured: same seed, score **3000.12 with the NaN vs
94.71 without**.

It is not usable:

- **One destination, no steering.** +/-inf coordinates *would* reach other corners,
  but no action can produce one - `move_distance` is clamped before use and
  `cos`/`sin` are bounded, so the arithmetic only ever yields finite or NaN.
- **No escape.** 1152 attempts (16 distances x 72 angles) produced one distinct
  position. Children spawn at the parent's exact position, are equally immobile,
  and are mortal.
- **It does not occupy predators.** `predator.step()` is a pure function of the
  current observation with no carried target. Measured: a predator mid-charge when
  its target teleports spends **0 of the next 3000 ticks** within hearing range of
  the corner.
- **It cannot cross the wire.** Starlette's `JSONResponse` uses `allow_nan=False`,
  so `agent_server.py` raises and returns 500, which the runner scores as a failed
  game. Emitting it requires hand-crafting a non-JSON-compliant body.

Recommendation: report it, do not ship it. One `math.isfinite` guard in
`update_entity_direction` plus a NaN check in `_keep_agent_in_bounds` closes it -
patch both engines together or lockstep breaks.

### A real risk to our own verification

`get_double` / `PyFloat_AsDouble` (`_engine.cpp:1687-1701`) coerces anything with
`__float__` or `__index__`. `np.float16` / `np.float32` are therefore accepted and
**silently break Python-vs-native lockstep**: `move_direction = np.float16(5)`
gives x = 269.5 in Python against 269.679 native, diverging chaotically from tick
one. `Decimal("NaN")` and any object whose `__float__` returns NaN reach the NaN
defect through the native path while the Python engine rejects them.

This is not a competition exploit - it is a gap in *our* guarantee. The
`verify_evasion.py` parity claim holds only while the policy emits plain Python
floats. Worth a type check at the boundary.

## 6. The one legal mechanism not yet tried

Agent radius 5, predator radius 10, touch range 15 - so there is geometry an agent
can occupy and a predator physically cannot. In a constructed 14-wide slot:

- 3000 ticks survived; closest predator approach **50** against a 15-unit kill range
- predator drained to 34 energy - below the 40 walk cap - and asleep **27%** of the
  time against **15%** for an unbaited control
- the agent issued no move at all, so movement cost was zero

Chase overrides wall avoidance (`predator.py:31-44` returns before the `if edges:`
branch), so a hooked predator jams indefinitely. Entry must be at sprint: walking
in from 390 units is caught at tick 9.

Two honest limits: natural pockets are **rare** (0.18-0.27% of agent-legal cells
on real maps, and reachability was not verified), and the pocket buys predator
safety but **not food** - a real agent still starves in ~66 s. Given that 82% of
deaths are starving agents, that caveat may be fatal to its value.

Separately measured and **not** worth doing: kiting. Fleeing costs the agent
5.60/tick against the predator's 2.03 - **2.77x worse for us** - and the predator
regenerates while resting whereas the agent never does.

## 7. What is still untested

Ranked by the strength of the argument behind them, not by expected size.

1. **`sprint_floor`, properly implemented.** Keep agents above `max_energy/5` by
   feeding locked agents and newborns first, rather than the `breed_reserve` proxy
   that failed. It is the only mechanism in the repo's history that demonstrably
   moved the dominant death category (kills 146 -> 77-90 per game), it scored
   positive on two independent 96-seed screens once its known failure mode was
   removed, and its 432-seed confirmation was queued and never reported. This is an
   unfinished experiment, not a new hypothesis.
2. **Sound tree-age bounds.** `_tree_value` treats *first sighting* as *birth*
   (`orchard_population.py:636`, `:568`), using literals `55.`, `58.`, `62.5` that
   are not in `self.P` and that **2410 trials could not reach**. A tree observed
   dropping fruit is provably >= 20 s old, which is free information already
   recorded in `t.fruit_seen`.
3. **Closed-loop population cap.** `_cap()` is an open-loop clock prior, identical
   on every map, while biome mix changes tree density ~10x. It also pins at
   `cap_min` from t ~ 1551 - almost exactly the mean survival time - so it stops
   tracking precisely where every game dies.
4. **Terminal provisioning concentration.** Score is the *last* death, so the tail
   is `max_i(energy_i)`, not the sum. `_assign_fruits` sorts hungriest-first
   (`:722`), which equalises remaining life and therefore *minimises* the maximum -
   correct while growing, wrong when terminal.
5. **Turning-circle orbit.** A predator moving 15/tick with a 0.3 rad/tick cap has
   a minimum turning radius ~50; inside it, it cannot close, and no speed is
   required - which is why it is the one geometry idea that could help the 82%.
   Prior controlled tests: 68.7 s vs 2.2 s survival, 12/12 runs >= 60 s vs 0/12.
   **Never run in a full game.** The design agent for this died on a rate limit
   before finishing; its work is worth resuming.

Explicitly **not** worth more compute, on measured evidence: wall deflection
(-54 to -135), `evade_closest` (-128), `cluster_radius` (-36 to -109), corner
steering, `pred_dodge_hold`, `evade_search`, `hide_mode`, `pred_share`, kiting.

## 8. Method that should carry forward

- 500 maps per candidate; 50 was noise-dominated and 200 is marginal.
- Every comparison paired against a control **on the same CPU family**.
- A calibration control in every sweep - a dial the engine certainly reads. If it
  does not move, the rig is broken and the nulls mean nothing.
- Separate seed bands per stage: training `0..499`, in-study validation `9000+`,
  ad-hoc held-out `5000..5199`, sweeps `20000+`.
- Verify mechanisms at their **on** value, never only at defaults.
- Confirm a launched job is actually running before reporting it as launched.
